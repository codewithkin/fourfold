# FourFold Marketing & ASO Strategy

## 1. Positioning and audience

### Positioning

**FourFold is a calm, fair, on-device daily word-grouping game:** players sort 16 words into four groups of four, receive an explanation for each solved group, and can return for unlimited local practice without an account, timer, or network connection after installation. The product is not a new mechanic; the 16-to-four format is already recognizable through products such as NYT Connections and other direct variants. [36] [37] FourFold therefore wins through the combination of:

- **A quiet daily ritual:** one Daily Four, a visible but forgiving local streak, and no obligation to keep playing.
- **Fair, explainable solving:** category labels and short explanations make the “aha” legible; gentle wrong-guess feedback and optional hints help players recover.
- **Private, low-friction ownership:** bundled content, local progress/stats/streaks, no account, and no server analytics in the MVP.
- **A small, polished experience:** responsive tile interactions, soft audio/haptics, readable boards, and short sessions.
- **Choice after the daily solve:** Practice and five themed packs for players who want more without changing the Daily Four streak.

The strategic line should not make “connection” the brand hero. Because the closest benchmark is named Connections and uses similar grouping language, the safer expression is a FourFold-owned promise such as **“Spot the pattern. Finish the four.”** or **“Four groups. One quiet reset.”** The mechanic should then be stated plainly: **“Group 16 words into four categories.”** This reduces confusion risk while retaining search relevance. Apple prohibits copycat or confusingly similar representations, and Google requires metadata and assets to be honest and not misleading. [4] [11]

### Priority audiences

| Audience | Need or tension | FourFold message | Best first channel/asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily word-game and puzzle players | Wants a satisfying mental reset without a long session | “One quiet daily puzzle: 16 words, four groups.” | Store page, Daily Four screenshot, creator solve clip |
| Cozy, calm, and low-pressure mobile players | Avoids timers, loud feedback, ads, and friction | “A calm word-grouping game that works offline.” | Short-form video, calm screenshot sequence |
| Book, film, food, and travel enthusiasts | Likes familiar vocabulary and themed discovery | “Practice by theme: five packs to explore.” | Pack screenshot, creator/UGC challenge |
| Privacy-conscious players | Does not want an account or cloud dependency | “Your progress and stats stay on your device.” | Store description, Settings/Privacy screenshot |
| Existing 16-to-four players seeking a different tone | Knows the mechanic but wants less pressure and more explanation | “Fair categories, gentle recovery, no required timer.” | Comparison-free creative; never use competitor names |

These are hypotheses to validate through store search behavior, reviews, creator reactions, and conversion tests—not claims about proven keyword volume. Practitioner ASO sources recommend starting from genre, mechanic, mood, and player language, then validating by market. [38] [39]

### Product/market promise

**One-sentence promise:**

> FourFold is a calm daily word-grouping puzzle where you find four fair categories in a 4×4 board, then keep playing offline through Practice and themed packs.

**Proof points available in the MVP:**

- 16 clear word tiles in a 4×4 board.
- Four categories to solve; correct groups lock with a category label and explanation. [33] [34]
- Daily Four selected from bundled content by the device date; a completed Daily Four cannot be replayed for a second daily result that day. [33] [34]
- Practice does not change Daily Four completion or streak. [33] [34]
- Five bundled themes: Food & Drink, Travel & Places, Movies & TV, Home & Everyday Life, and Nature & Animals. [33] [34]
- Local stats: completed puzzles, current/best streak, average mistakes, hints used, and completion rate. [33]
- Optional local reminders, independent sound/music/haptic controls, and native result sharing. [33] [34]
- No account, backend, cloud sync, remote content, payments, subscriptions, or server analytics in the MVP. [33]

**Do not promise:** a globally synchronized challenge, new content forever, cross-device progress, online leaderboards, an account archive, multiplayer, live events, or server-side social/referral attribution. The MVP uses bundled date-selected content and local state, so the marketing claim is **“play offline after install”**, not “nothing ever leaves your device.” The OS share sheet, linked legal pages, app stores, and any future SDK sit outside the game’s local storage boundary. [13] [14] [33]

## 2. Brand voice and creative identity

### Voice

FourFold should sound **clear, warm, observant, and lightly witty**. Use short sentences, concrete mechanics, and an inviting tone. Make the player feel capable rather than judged.

**Use:**

- “Spot the pattern.”
- “Take a quiet minute.”
- “Try four words.”
- “A fair category, when you see it.”
- “Practice by theme.”
- “Your streak lives on this device.”
- “A hint is a nudge, not a penalty.”

**Avoid:**

- “Beat the clock,” “crush the puzzle,” or “don’t break your streak.”
- “Best,” “#1,” “addictive,” “brain training,” or unsupported cognitive/health claims.
- Competitor names such as NYT, Connections, Wordle, or Red Herring in metadata or creative.
- “New puzzle every day forever,” “infinite content,” or “play anywhere with sync.”
- Fake reviews, rankings, install/price calls to action, or comparative claims.

The product tone should be calm but not sleepy: the interaction sound brief describes soft transients, warm ascending notes for correct groups, and a muted wrong-guess cue rather than an alarm. [35]

### Visual identity

Build a distinct system around **four rounded tiles or four interlocking shapes**, not the familiar yellow/green/blue/purple visual language, tile proportions, typography, or share-card treatment of a close competitor. [36] [11]

- Warm off-white and ink neutrals establish the board.
- One FourFold accent carries primary actions and selected states.
- Four secondary category accents identify groups, but every state also has a label, border, pattern, icon, or position cue so meaning is never color-only. Apple advises consistent semantic color and not relying on color alone; Android recommends accessible contrast and 48dp touch targets. [19] [20]
- Use one readable sans-serif family with Regular, Medium, and Semibold weights.
- Keep motion low-amplitude: selection scale, a short wrong-guess shake, a clean group lock, and a gentle completion reveal.
- Test long words, larger text, dark mode, increased contrast, and localized strings before capturing store assets. [19] [20]

### Icon direction

Create three genuinely different icon concepts using the same FourFold family:

1. **Tile grid:** four rounded tiles forming a compact square, with one subtle offset or highlighted tile.
2. **Negative-space link:** four blocks whose gaps create a quiet interlocking mark.
3. **Four-block F:** an abstract F constructed from four geometric blocks.

The icon should be centered, legible at thumbnail size, and contain no tiny wordmark. Apple’s icon guidance favors a simple, recognizable, centered concept with little or no text and a 1024×1024 source for iOS/iPadOS/macOS. [18] Test the three concepts with store-native experiments rather than team preference.

## 3. Store-page messaging and ASO

### Store-page principles

The first view must explain the mechanic without requiring the description. Lead with the real 4×4 board, readable words, and a concise benefit. Apple recommends that early assets focus on the core experience; Google requires actual gameplay and concise, accurate metadata. [15] [17]

Keep the brand lead plus descriptive mechanic terms. “FourFold” alone is not a useful search explanation. Conversely, do not turn descriptions into keyword blocks: Google prohibits repetitive or misleading metadata, and Apple advises against duplicating the app/company name or using competing app names in keywords. [4] [5]

### Apple draft metadata

Apple currently documents an app name and subtitle of up to 30 characters each, a promotional text field of up to 170 characters, a description of up to 4,000 characters, and a keyword field limited to 100 bytes. Apple says searchability includes app name, subtitle, keywords, and company name. [1] [2]

**Recommended launch version (validate trademark and availability first):**

- **Name:** `FourFold: Word Groups` (22 characters)
- **Subtitle:** `Calm daily word puzzles` (23 characters)
- **Promotional text:** `Group 16 words into four fair categories. Play the Daily Four, practice by theme, and keep your progress on your device.`
- **Keywords, starting hypothesis:** `word,group,puzzle,daily,logic,brain,category,offline,practice`

The keyword string above is 61 ASCII bytes before any locale-specific changes. Treat it as a starting hypothesis, not a ranking guarantee. Remove any term duplicated in the name/company name, avoid competitor names, and validate by locale with App Store Search and Search Ads/Search Match where available. Apple documents a 100-byte limit and keyword exclusions, but not a complete ranking formula. [2] [5]

**Apple description draft:**

> Find the pattern. Finish the four.
>
> FourFold is a calm word-grouping puzzle built around a simple challenge: sort 16 words into four groups of four. Each group has a category to discover, and each solved group comes with a clear explanation.
>
> Start with the Daily Four, a short local puzzle selected for your device date. Finish it to update your local streak, then keep playing without pressure in Practice or explore five themed packs: Food & Drink, Travel & Places, Movies & TV, Home & Everyday Life, and Nature & Animals.
>
> FourFold is designed for a quiet, satisfying session:
>
> - Read a clean 4×4 word board.
> - Select four words and test a connection.
> - Shuffle the unsolved tiles when a new view helps.
> - Use an optional hint when you need a nudge.
> - Get gentle feedback for a wrong guess and a clear explanation for a correct group.
> - See mistakes, hints, completion time, streak impact, and local stats.
> - Share a spoiler-safe result through your device’s share sheet.
>
> No account is required. After installation, bundled puzzles, progress, streaks, stats, settings, and optional reminders work on your device. Practice never changes your Daily Four streak. There is no required timer, server leaderboard, cloud save, or online connection needed to play.
>
> FourFold is a small daily reset for word lovers, puzzle fans, and anyone who enjoys finding the idea that brings four words together.

The description must remain synchronized with the shipped build and privacy policy. Apple requires accurate metadata and actual in-app representation. [11]

**Apple custom product pages at launch:** use three focused pages, not a large speculative portfolio. Apple supports up to 70 custom product pages with distinct assets, promotional text, and keyword assignments, plus unique URLs and acquisition reporting. [7]

| Page | Audience intent | First-frame message | Keyword subset | Destination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily Four | Daily puzzle/habit | “One quiet puzzle for today.” | `daily,puzzle,streak` | Daily creator posts, launch page |
| Calm/offline | Low-pressure/privacy | “Word puzzles, offline and on-device.” | `offline,logic,practice` | Privacy-conscious communities, paid test |
| Themed packs | Category curiosity | “Practice by theme.” | `category,word,groups` | Food/travel/book/film creative |

Assign a page only to traffic whose creative actually matches it. Apple page analytics require enough first-time downloads to be useful; do not treat a low-volume page as a definitive winner. [7]

Google Play documents a title of no more than 30 characters, a short description of no more than 80 characters, and a full description of no more than 4,000 characters. Google asks for concise, accurate, natural copy and prohibits repetitive keyword blocks, misleading references, ranking claims, and promotional/price claims in metadata and graphics. [3] [4]

**Recommended launch version:**

- **Title:** `FourFold: Word Groups` (22 characters)
- **Short description:** `Group 16 words into four. Calm daily puzzles, themed packs and practice.` (72 characters)

**Alternative title hypothesis for later testing:** `FourFold: Word Group Puzzle` (28 characters). Do not change the default listing repeatedly before there is enough traffic to interpret results.

**Google full description draft:**

> FourFold is a calm daily word puzzle about finding the pattern between words.
>
> Group 16 words into four categories on a clear 4×4 board. Select four tiles, submit your guess, and reveal a group with its category explanation. A wrong guess gives you gentle feedback so you can look again; an optional hint can help when you are stuck.
>
> Play the Daily Four for a small daily ritual and a local streak. When you want more, choose Practice or explore five themed packs:
>
> • Food & Drink
> • Travel & Places
> • Movies & TV
> • Home & Everyday Life
> • Nature & Animals
>
> FourFold is made for short, low-pressure sessions. There is no required timer. Shuffle the unsolved tiles, solve at your own pace, then see your mistakes, hints, completion time, and streak impact on the result screen. Local Stats show completed puzzles, current and best streaks, average mistakes, hints used, and completion rate.
>
> Your play stays simple and private. No account is required. After installation, the bundled puzzles, progress, stats, streaks, settings, and optional Daily Four reminder stay on your device. FourFold works offline and Practice never changes your Daily Four streak. Share a spoiler-safe result through the Android Sharesheet when you want to show how you did.
>
> Find four fair groups. Take a quiet minute. Play FourFold.

**Google custom listings:** start with two: a Daily Four/streak listing for campaign URLs and a themed-pack listing for relevant creative. Later, use one search-keyword listing for a high-intent phrase such as “word grouping puzzle” only after search data exists. Google supports custom store listings for countries, search keywords, ads traffic, custom audiences, lapsed/churned segments, and unique URLs; it supports listing experiments with icons, feature graphics, screenshots, and descriptions. [9] [10]

### Keyword strategy

Use a three-bucket system and keep each platform’s text natural:

| Bucket | Candidate terms | Where to validate | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanic | word groups, word grouping, group words, four groups, 16 words, category puzzle, word association | Apple Search, Search Match, Play search terms, reviews | Explain the actual 16-to-four mechanic; do not imply a different game |
| Intent | daily word puzzle, logic puzzle, practice puzzle, offline word game, short puzzle | Store autocomplete, listing experiments, campaign landing pages | Treat volume and conversion as hypotheses |
| Mood/benefit | calm, quiet, low-pressure, no timer, no account, on-device | Review language, creator comments, qualitative tests | Use only claims true in the release; avoid “brain training” efficacy claims |

**Apple:** use the 100-byte field for distinct, non-duplicative terms; do not waste it on the brand or competitor names. Localize the field rather than translating English terms mechanically. [2] [5]

**Google:** place one or two mechanic terms naturally in the title/short description and use the full description to explain features. Do not repeat phrases unnaturally or create keyword blocks. Google discovery uses relevance, quality, engagement, ratings/reviews, and surface-dependent personalization; neither Google nor Apple publishes a stable complete weighting formula. [4] [6]

**Measurement:** compare impressions, product-page/listing conversion, downloads, and review language. Do not claim a keyword “works” from impressions alone; require a meaningful conversion or qualitative signal. Store analytics are aggregate and cannot replace in-app cohorts in this serverless MVP. [26] [27]

## 4. Screenshot, icon, video, and share creative strategy

### Core screenshot set

Create six real-UI frames, with the first three doing most of the conversion work:

1. **Mechanic:** real 4×4 board, 16 readable words, four tiles selected. Overlay: **“16 words. Four groups.”**
2. **Aha:** one correct group locking with category label and short explanation. Overlay: **“Every group has an aha.”**
3. **Proof of completion:** results screen with groups, mistakes, hints, time, and streak. Overlay: **“A quiet win, clearly explained.”**
4. **Habit:** Home Daily Four card showing ready/in-progress/complete states and local streak. Overlay: **“A daily puzzle, no pressure.”**
5. **Depth:** Packs screen showing all five launch themes and Practice. Overlay: **“Practice by theme.”**
6. **Ownership:** Stats and/or Settings showing local stats, reminder controls, and on-device progress. Overlay: **“Your progress stays on your device.”**

Use no fingers, device mockups, stock photography, fake chat, or functionality outside the MVP. Apple supports up to 10 screenshots and recommends a cohesive story led by the strongest feature; Google recommends actual gameplay early and concise screenshot taglines. [15] [17]

For Google, prepare at least the required minimum and a recommended set of four high-resolution portrait screenshots; keep overlays short and avoid calls to action, ranking claims, prices, and promotional language. [17]

### Preview video

Produce one 20–24 second portrait master plus platform-safe crops/localized versions:

- **0–2s:** real board appears; caption “16 words. Four groups.”
- **2–7s:** select four words; show immediate visual/audio confirmation.
- **7–11s:** submit; reveal category and explanation.
- **11–14s:** show one gentle wrong-guess recovery or hint.
- **14–19s:** solve remaining groups; show calm completion state.
- **19–24s:** Daily Four card, local streak, and native Share Result.

Start with gameplay, caption all meaning for muted autoplay, and keep at least 80% representative gameplay for Google. Apple allows app previews up to 30 seconds and advises app-only footage, gameplay-first storytelling, a beginning/middle/end, and legible copy for muted playback. [16] Google recommends core gameplay within the first 10 seconds and short, captioned videos. [17]

Also test **video versus screenshot-first** rather than assuming video wins; Apple’s own optimization examples include a case where a page without a preview video outperformed the video treatment. [8]

### Share result

Keep the MVP text-first and spoiler-safe:

> FourFold • Daily Four • 4/4 groups • 1 mistake • 0 hints • 03:42 • streak 7
>
> Can you solve today’s four?

Use the native iOS activity view and Android Sharesheet. If an image card is added later, use a warm neutral background, four labeled/numbered result rows, date/status, mistakes, hints, time, and streak. Do not add leaderboards, referral rewards, social profiles, or online-sync language. [34]

## 5. Localization plan

### Launch sequence

1. **English launch:** ship the full product, store metadata, screenshots, video captions, landing page, Privacy/Terms, and first bundled puzzle in the same language.
2. **Localization readiness:** externalize all UI strings, screenshot overlays, preview captions, pack names/descriptions, onboarding, hint copy, result/share text, notification copy, and legal/support text.
3. **Second locale only after evidence:** use store search terms, conversion, reviews, and qualitative feedback to choose the next market. Do not assume direct translations preserve search behavior.
4. **Locale QA:** test long words in the 4×4 board, category explanation wrapping, larger accessibility text, dark mode, text-to-speech labels, date formatting, and local-date rollover.

Translate the player-facing words and puzzle corpus, not just metadata. A localized store page that opens into English puzzles will create an expectation gap. For Movies & TV, use original or properly licensed words/artwork and generic examples in creatives unless rights to recognizable titles, logos, characters, music, or clips are confirmed. Apple and Google require truthful, authorized assets. [11] [4]

## 6. Launch phases and channel strategy

### Launch essentials versus later work

**Required for launch:**

- Stable tutorial, first puzzle, results/share flow, date rollover, local persistence, and long-word/accessibility behavior.
- Six screenshots, icon candidates, store video, metadata, support URL, Privacy/Terms, age/content declarations, and store Data safety/App Privacy answers.
- 15–30 player closed beta with a direct feedback channel.
- One simple marketing/press page on `/apps/web` with store links, gameplay clip, privacy explanation, and support contact.
- A small creator/community/short-form pilot with honest disclosure and no review incentives.
- Store dashboard measurement and a written hypothesis for the first experiment.

**Later, only if evidence justifies it:**

- More Apple custom pages or Google custom listings.
- More locales and market-specific screenshots.
- Universal Links/App Links for result URLs.
- Image share cards after text sharing proves useful.
- Larger creator partnerships or paid media.
- Opt-in diagnostics or analytics; these are a new privacy/product decision, not a launch requirement.

### Phase 0: build and beta, weeks −4 to −3

- Freeze the MVP promise and remove unsupported copy.
- Audit the Expo/React Native dependency tree and release build for network calls, identifiers, crash reporting, ads, attribution, authentication, and diagnostics.
- Run TestFlight external testing and Google Play closed testing with 15–30 representative players. Apple supports internal/external testers and feedback; Google supports internal, closed, and open testing and says test feedback does not affect the public rating. [22] [23]
- Ask structured questions: tutorial under one minute, first-puzzle fairness, ambiguity, hint usefulness, text wrapping, offline restart, date change, share wording, reminder timing, sound/haptic comfort, and crashes.
- Build and QA the landing/press kit; do not create an email waitlist unless the team intentionally adds a third-party data processor and revises privacy copy.

### Phase 1: store readiness, weeks −3 to −1

- Finalize icon, screenshots, video, metadata, privacy policy, support destination, age rating, target audience/content declarations, and content/IP review.
- If timing and content are firm, use Apple pre-order where eligible and Google pre-registration for a 3–6 week window; these are acquisition surfaces, not substitutes for a polished playable build. [24] [25]
- Prepare 8–12 short clips and three creator briefs.
- Recruit 10–20 small, relevant creators and request honest gameplay reactions rather than guaranteed positive reviews.
- Prepare a personalized press note, GamesPress submission, and moderator-permission community posts.

### Phase 2: launch week

- Release with the default store page and only the initial focused custom pages/listings that have real destinations.
- Publish one creator wave, 3–4 short-form posts, one approved community contribution per community, one press submission, and the launch-page update.
- Monitor crashes, support questions, store conversion, review themes, tutorial confusion, and Daily Four completion feedback.
- Do not change multiple store assets in the first few days unless a factual or policy error is found.

### Phase 3: weeks +1 to +4

- Run one store experiment at a time: icon first, then screenshot 1 or first-three sequence, then video versus screenshot-first.
- Continue the best-performing content family among calm ritual, aha/reveal, and creator reaction.
- Fix ambiguity, accessibility, and notification complaints before adding new claims.
- Use Apple Product Page Optimization and Google Store Listing Experiments where eligible; Apple recommends waiting for a high-confidence result, and Google recommends at least a week to cover weekday/weekend patterns. [8] [10]

### Phase 4: weeks +5 to +12

- Expand the strongest locale or campaign page only after the default promise converts and the product experience is stable.
- Refresh screenshots only when the product or player language changes.
- Add a second creator/community cohort, not a large unmeasured spend.
- Decide whether image share cards, deep links, additional pages, or a second locale are worth the engineering/privacy cost.

### Organic channel strategy

**Short-form video:** publish 3–4 vertical clips per week for four weeks, cross-posting the core footage to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Use 9:16, captions, UI-safe placement, a proposition in the first seconds, and real gameplay. TikTok recommends vertical mobile-first creative, early hooks, captions, creator/customer presence, and continuous testing; treat this as platform guidance, not an install forecast. [21]

Content formats:

- **Connection reveal:** four words, ask viewers to pause/comment, then reveal the category.
- **“I was sure it was…”:** real wrong guess, gentle shake, reframe, solution.
- **One-minute reset:** Daily Four on a quiet desk/commute/coffee moment; promise a short session, not a fixed completion time.
- **Theme curiosity:** one original Food & Drink, Travel & Places, Home & Everyday Life, or Nature & Animals puzzle. For Movies & TV, avoid unauthorized titles and footage.
- **Share-safe result:** show the result format without spoiling the category.

**Owned web:** `/apps/web` should contain the promise, gameplay clip, store links, privacy explanation, support/contact destination, and press kit. If a result URL is introduced later, use normal HTTPS and let installed apps open the content while uninstalled users see the web fallback; this is optional later work, not an MVP acquisition dependency. [31]

**Paid media:** start with small, time-boxed tests only after the organic/store page communicates clearly. Use one creative family per campaign and route traffic to the matching Apple custom page or Google custom listing. Optimize to store page conversion and acquisition cost where the platform reports it; do not infer in-app retention or referral coefficient without server analytics.

## 7. Creator, community, and PR plan

### Creator/UGC brief

Seed 10–20 small, relevant creators across word-game/puzzle, cozy mobile, books, film, food, travel, and accessibility communities. Give each one an unrevealed puzzle and allow face-cam, voiceover, or screen-only capture.

Ask creators to show:

1. Their first grouping thought.
2. One real wrong guess or hint decision.
3. The category explanation.
4. The completed result and honest reaction.

Opening prompt: **“What would you group?”** rather than a hard-sell download line. Prohibit spoilers in the opening frame, fake reactions, competitor comparisons, and unlicensed Movies & TV assets. If a creator receives money, free access, a benefit, or another material connection, require clear platform-appropriate disclosure; TikTok, YouTube, and FTC guidance all emphasize disclosure and truthful endorsements. [30]

Evaluate creator pilots by landing-page/store visits, attributed installs where available, comment quality, and audience fit—not views alone. There is no credible FourFold-specific low-budget ROI benchmark for creator seeding; treat it as a controlled pilot.

### Community

Start with 5–8 permission-based communities that genuinely fit: word games, puzzles, cozy games, books/film, travel, and mobile indie. Read rules first, ask moderators before beta/launch posts, contribute a playable daily challenge or puzzle discussion, disclose that FourFold is yours, and post infrequently. Do not mass-DM, cross-post repetitively, or manufacture engagement. Reddit prohibits repeated/unsolicited mass engagement and Discord prohibits unsolicited bulk messages and inauthentic engagement. [28] [29]

### PR

Create a lightweight press kit containing:

- 50-word and 200-word descriptions.
- Logo and icon.
- Six clean screenshots.
- 15-second vertical gameplay and 30-second horizontal gameplay.
- Release date, platforms, founder/support contact.
- Fact sheet: offline after install, no account, local stats/streaks, five launch packs, no server analytics.

Pitch a short list of mobile, puzzle, cozy, and accessibility journalists with a personalized, embargoed note. Submit the same factual materials to GamesPress, understanding that submission does not guarantee coverage. [32]

## 8. Retention, notifications, and review loop

### Retention architecture

Keep the loop centered on **Daily Four → calm result/streak update → optional share → Practice or Packs**. Daily Four is a local date-selected puzzle; Practice never changes the daily streak; packs and stats provide non-streak reasons to return. [33] [34]

Make the streak forgiving:

- Show current and best streak without shame.
- Do not lock content, charge for recovery, erase Practice progress, or use guilt copy.
- Explain that streak state is local and a missed day starts a new run.
- Handle device-date changes without duplicate Daily results or crashes. [34]

Research on streaks and daily rewards is mixed: streak incentives can increase persistence in task settings, while players can also experience daily mechanics as obligation or FOMO. Treat gentle streak presentation as a product hypothesis and validate through qualitative feedback rather than maximizing pressure. [40] [41]

### Reminder policy

Offer one optional local Daily Four reminder with a player-selected time and neutral copy such as **“Your Daily Four is ready.”** Do not call it time-sensitive or send catch-up spam. Explain that it is a local device notification, then request OS permission from Settings after the player enables the setting—not on splash, Welcome, the tutorial, or the first puzzle. Apple and Android both recommend contextual, user-controlled permission timing. [42] [43]

### Review prompt policy

Request a rating only after demonstrated value: after several completed puzzles including at least one Daily Four, on a calm results screen after the completion animation. Never prompt after a wrong guess, game over, reminder denial, onboarding, or a permission flow.

Use the native Apple review API and Google Play in-app review flow. Do not create a sentiment gate, custom imitation, reward, forced prompt, or “rate us five stars” language. Store prompts are quota-controlled and may not appear; keep a local cooldown of at least 14 days and no more than once per app version unless deliberately changed. Apple and Google both prohibit incentivized or deceptive review manipulation. [12] [44] [45]

### Support and review response

Respond to substantive reviews with empathy and a concrete fix or explanation. Tag recurring themes locally in a release spreadsheet: ambiguity/fairness, readability, notification behavior, offline expectations, sharing, crashes, and sound/haptic comfort. Use those themes to improve puzzles and store copy, not to solicit only positive ratings.

## 9. Measurement and KPIs

FourFold has no server analytics, so measurement must be explicit about what is and is not observable.

### Store and campaign metrics

| Funnel stage | KPI | Source | Decision use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discoverability | Impressions, search/browse share, custom-page/listing impressions | App Store Connect, Play Console | Is the message being surfaced? |
| Consideration | Product-page/listing view rate, screenshot/video engagement where available | Store dashboards | Are assets earning attention? |
| Acquisition | Conversion to download, first-time downloads, attributed campaign traffic | Store dashboards, campaign URLs/UTMs | Which audience/page converts? |
| Quality | Ratings, review themes, crash/support complaints | Store reviews, platform tools, manual support | What breaks the promise? |
| Early experience | Opt-in beta feedback, tutorial completion observations, first-puzzle completion feedback | TestFlight/closed testing, structured form/email | Is onboarding and fairness working? |
| Content | Views, watch-through, comments, saves, link/store visits | TikTok/Meta/YouTube dashboards | Which creative family merits another pilot? |

Apple reports acquisition sources, impressions, product-page views, downloads, and conversion; Google reports acquisition/traffic sources and supports campaign dimensions such as UTM source/campaign. These are aggregate store measures, not user-level in-app cohorts. [26] [27]

### Guardrails

- Do not add telemetry solely to calculate D1/D7/D28 retention. Industry retention benchmarks are directional and not a forecast for a serverless word game. [46]
- Do not call a creative a winner from impressions alone.
- Use one major variable per store experiment; record hypothesis, audience, start/end date, primary metric, guardrail, and stop rule.
- For store decisions, prioritize conversion, then review/support quality and qualitative puzzle completion feedback.
- Treat local Stats as a player feature and QA aid, not a growth dashboard.

| E2 | Mechanic-first beats mood-first for cold traffic | Screenshot 1: “16 words. Four groups.” vs “A calm daily puzzle.” | Apple PPO/Google | Conversion | One asset family at a time; retain factual winner |
| E3 | Showing explanation increases trust | First three screenshots: mechanic → explanation → result vs mechanic → Daily Four → packs | Both | Conversion plus review themes | Keep the sequence that reduces “what is this?” feedback |
| E4 | Video earns incremental understanding | Preview video vs strong screenshot-first page | Both where eligible | Conversion | Do not assume video wins; Apple examples show it may not |
| E5 | Offline/on-device message attracts a distinct audience | Store page copy and first screenshot emphasis | Custom pages/listings | Campaign conversion | Use only if shipped build and disclosures remain accurate |
| E6 | Daily habit framing improves campaign conversion | Daily Four/streak custom page vs default | Apple custom page, Google custom listing | Page/listing conversion | Use only for daily-intent traffic |
| E7 | Theme-specific creative expands qualified traffic | Themed-pack page with actual five packs | Apple/Google custom destination | Conversion and comments | Do not infer pack demand from views alone |
| E8 | Reveal clips drive higher-quality engagement | Connection reveal vs wrong-guess recovery vs quiet reset | TikTok/Reels/Shorts | Watch-through, comments, store visits | Recut only the strongest hook; do not buy scale before repeatability |
| E9 | Localized player language improves relevance | English-market copy vs localized mechanic/mood copy | Store locale experiment | Conversion and review language | Expand one locale at a time |

## 11. Privacy, policy, and compliance

### MVP data boundary

The shipped architecture supports a low-data posture only if the final dependency and release-build audit confirms it. FourFold’s gameplay, progress, stats, streaks, settings, and reminder preferences can remain on-device; Apple does not define on-device-only processing as collected data when it is never transmitted to a server. [13] The claim must be rechecked for every Expo/native dependency, because crash reporting, ads, attribution, authentication, social, diagnostics, or remote-config SDKs can change disclosures. Google requires Data safety for every published app, including apps that collect no data, and requires third-party libraries/SDK behavior to be included. [14]

### Required launch controls

- Keep a publicly reachable Privacy Policy and Terms page on `/apps/web` and a real Support URL for Apple.
- State precisely: no FourFold account, backend, cloud save, or server analytics; bundled content and local progress; reset deletes covered local records; Share Result hands chosen text to the OS share sheet and the recipient app.
- Distinguish the native app from browser traffic to linked legal pages and user-directed share recipients.
- Complete Apple App Privacy and Google Data safety from the final build, not from architectural intent. Re-audit after every SDK/module change. [13] [14]
- Complete Apple age rating from the actual corpus and Google target-audience/content declarations from actual launch content. Do not market to children merely to widen reach; child-appealing listings can trigger additional requirements. [47]
- Review Movies & TV words, logos, artwork, quotes, music, and screenshots for rights before they appear in the app or store creative. Use generic category examples unless rights are cleared. [11] [4]
- Keep screenshots, video, metadata, and notification language accurate to the current build. Do not show cloud sync, a timer, accounts, online content, or infinite daily supply.

## 12. 90-day action plan

### Days 1–14: scope lock and quality gate

- Freeze the FourFold promise and remove competitor-adjacent “connection” hero language.
- Audit content fairness, ambiguity, rights, and launch corpus.
- Test tutorial, first puzzle, hint, wrong guess, date rollover, offline restart, local reset, share cancellation, long words, dark mode, contrast, and touch targets.
- Audit Expo/native dependencies and build network behavior.
- Draft final Privacy Policy, Terms, Support page, and store disclosures.
- Produce icon concepts and screenshot wireframes.

**Exit gate:** a new player can understand and complete the first puzzle without network, login, or permission prompt; no launch copy contradicts MVP scope.

### Days 15–30: beta and asset production

- Recruit 15–30 TestFlight/Google closed-test players.
- Run structured feedback and fix tutorial, fairness, readability, sound/haptic, reminder, and share issues.
- Capture real UI for six screenshots and a 20–24 second preview.
- Build `/apps/web` launch/press page with store links and privacy explanation.
- Prepare Apple and Google metadata drafts, localized English copy, age/content forms, and three Apple/two Google focused destinations.

**Exit gate:** no high-severity crash, blank/error state, misleading screenshot, or unresolved rights question.

### Days 31–45: pre-launch distribution

- Publish Google pre-registration for a 3–6 week window if the release date is firm; use Apple pre-order only if eligible and operationally useful. [24] [25]
- Start 3–4 short-form posts per week.
- Brief 10–20 small creators; provide honest preview access and disclosure requirements.
- Ask permission in selected communities; submit the press kit to a targeted journalist list and GamesPress.
- Finalize campaign links, custom page/listing mapping, support response templates, and store dashboards.

**Exit gate:** every campaign link lands on a page whose first creative matches the audience promise.

### Days 46–60: launch

- Release with the default page plus only validated focused destinations.
- Coordinate creator, community, PR, and short-form wave.
- Watch conversion, support, review themes, crashes, tutorial comprehension, and Daily Four feedback daily.
- Request native reviews only after demonstrated value; do not ask beta testers for public reviews.

**Exit gate:** resolve any store-policy, privacy, crash, or fairness issue before increasing spend or expanding locales.

### Days 61–75: first optimization cycle

- Run E1 icon test.
- If sufficient traffic exists, run E2 screenshot-1 test; otherwise wait rather than over-read noise.
- Recut the strongest organic hook and stop weak formats.
- Publish a factual update based on player feedback, not feature inflation.

### Days 76–90: scale what is proven

- Run one of E3–E7 based on observed search/campaign language.
- Expand one locale or creator cohort only if the default experience remains stable.
- Decide whether image share cards, deep links, more custom pages, or opt-in diagnostics are worth the cost and privacy implications.
- Write a 90-day review: what converted, what players misunderstood, which puzzle themes were requested, and which claims should be removed or strengthened.

## 13. Explicit risks and anti-patterns

| Risk/anti-pattern | Why it is dangerous | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| “Find the connection” as the only brand hook | Semantically adjacent to a dominant named competitor and its framing | Use a FourFold-owned headline; state the mechanic below it |
| Copying competitor colors, tile geometry, typography, result grids, or share cards | Can create confusion, copycat, or review risk | Build a distinct visual system and document design decisions |
| Claiming “new puzzle every day forever” | MVP uses bundled date-selected content | Say “Daily Four” and “bundled offline puzzles” |
| Adding analytics/ad/crash SDKs silently | Changes App Privacy, Data safety, and Privacy Policy obligations | Audit dependencies and release builds; re-submit disclosures when behavior changes |
| Requiring account/cloud sync language | Contradicts the no-account, local-only MVP | Say “progress stays on your device”; disclose reinstall/device-transfer limits where relevant |
| Notification prompt on first launch or guilt copy | Poor consent, permission denial, and calm-product mismatch | Explain local reminders in Settings; ask after user action |
| Rating prompt after a wrong guess or at onboarding | Interruptive and policy-sensitive; asks before demonstrated value | Native API only after successful solves; local cooldown |
| Buying positive reviews or rewarding ratings | Prohibited review manipulation and untrustworthy feedback | Ask for honest feedback; never attach rewards |
| Keyword stuffing or competitor names | Weak relevance, policy risk, and misleading metadata | Use natural mechanic terms; validate by market |
| Generic screenshots with mockups or stock lifestyle images | Fails actual-experience guidance and obscures the mechanic | Use real board, result, packs, stats, and settings |
| Overly quiet beige creative | Calm becomes unreadable at thumbnail size | High contrast, strong selected state, non-color cues, accessibility QA |
| Using recognizable Movies & TV IP without clearance | Copyright/trademark and platform-review exposure | Use original/generic terms or obtain rights before store use |
| Treating platform guidance as guaranteed performance | TikTok, Apple, Google, and vendors publish recommendations, not FourFold outcomes | Run controlled pilots and report uncertainty |
| Overbuilding custom pages, locales, or paid campaigns before signal | Splits low traffic and prevents interpretation | Start with three Apple pages/two Google listings and one-variable tests |
| Measuring store conversion as in-app retention | Serverless MVP cannot observe user-level cohorts | Use store metrics, opt-in beta feedback, reviews, and support; do not invent cohort data |
